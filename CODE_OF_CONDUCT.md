# Code of Conduct

## Scope

This policy applies to project spaces and activities, including issues, pull requests, code review, discussions, and project-related public communications. Everyone participating is expected to help keep these spaces respectful, constructive, and safe.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, patient, and constructive when disagreeing.
- Focus criticism on ideas and work, not on personal traits or identity.
- Make room for different experience levels, backgrounds, and communication styles.
- Respect privacy. Do not publish another person's personal information, private communications, credentials, prompts, or security-report details without permission.
- Accept feedback and follow maintainer requests to keep project spaces usable.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, discrimination, threats, sustained personal attacks, sexualized conduct or unwanted advances, doxxing, deliberate intimidation, and publishing private or sensitive information are not acceptable. This also includes abusive conduct in project-related spaces outside the repository when it targets a participant because of their project involvement.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to a repository maintainer through a private contact channel. If no suitable private channel is available, ask a maintainer to arrange one without posting sensitive details publicly. Do not include credentials, personal data, or vulnerability details in a public issue. Reports will be handled with discretion; absolute confidentiality cannot be promised when information is necessary to investigate or respond to an immediate safety or legal concern.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will review reports fairly and consider context, impact, pattern, and the safety of affected people. They may clarify expectations, request removal or correction of content, issue a warning, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently exclude a participant from project spaces. Enforcement should be proportionate to the conduct and may be adjusted if new information becomes available. Maintainers may remove content or restrict access promptly when needed to protect participants or sensitive information.

A maintainer who is directly involved in a report should not be its sole reviewer. If no uninvolved maintainer is available, the report should be referred to an appropriate trusted reviewer before a final enforcement decision where practicable.
